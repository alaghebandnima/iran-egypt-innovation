Estimation methods and validation

  1. Distance-weighted average: interpolation between neighboring observations; at a boundary, the three nearest observations are weighted by inverse distance.
  2. Regularized local trend: distance-weighted linear fit to five nearest observations; slope penalty 0.3.
  3. Trend curve: regularized quadratic after log1p for nonnegative values or inverse hyperbolic sine for signed values; slope and curvature penalties 0.05 and 0.5.
  4. Latent state: level and slope with a Kalman filter and backward smoother. Robust training differences set variance; level, slope and observation multipliers are 0.25, 0.02 and 0.15. These are fixed project settings, not maximum-likelihood estimates.

Artificial missingness validation hides known source values in contiguous windows with matching gap direction and length. All four methods use the same windows, at least three training observations, and up to eight spread-out folds. Trailing gaps use past values only; leading gaps use remaining future values; internal gaps use both sides. Hidden values never enter fitting, scaling or variance calibration. The lowest mean absolute error wins. When no matched window is available, shorter or single-point tests are explicitly reported; extrapolation to longer gaps is less certain.

1788 method–point comparisons were run (not independent observations). Independent nested validation covered the first 12 eligible patterns and 72 points. Mean absolute error divided by mean absolute training value was 20.67%. This is not an accuracy percentage or a guarantee for all gaps.

The three-year trailing R&D gap for Iran was validated using two-year windows because a matched three-year test was unavailable; applying that result to three years requires greater caution.
